THE PHILOSOPHY

Farm the path. Protect the baseline. Let the market pay you for waiting.

Baseline Farmer is built around a simple idea: you do not have to predict every move to think clearly about a market that moves above and below a level of exposure you actually want to own.

The long cycle

The larger crypto cycle provides the long-term reason to remain engaged with the asset. The halving is part of that thesis: it helps explain why another major expansion can eventually occur.

But the halving is not itself a trading signal, and the strategy does not require sitting idle for four or five years waiting for an all-time high.

A large cycle contains smaller advances, retreats, recoveries and extensions. The purpose of farming is to make those movements useful while retaining the longer-term position.

The long cycle provides the horizon. The path between major cycle points provides the opportunities.

The baseline

Your baseline is the amount of crypto exposure you want to maintain under normal conditions.

It is a target amount of exposure. It is not a market price, a support line, or a floor that the market is expected to respect.

Rebuild territory

Your actual exposure is below your target. The difference tells you how much exposure has not yet been established or has been lost through a decline.

That gap alone does not mean the market is offering a dip.

Harvest territory

Your actual exposure is above the target. The excess can be considered for realization while the desired baseline remains intact.

This does not require knowing exactly where the top is.

Entry is the first farming cycle

The entry process is not a separate investment strategy that gets discarded once the position is funded. It is the same farming framework operating before the baseline has been fully established.

How much position still needs to be built?

Target exposure, current exposure, available capital and remaining gap describe the position being built.

These are capital-management facts.

Is the market giving us a useful opportunity?

Price action, structural references, volume, volatility, historical behavior and cycle context describe what the market is doing.

These determine whether available capital should actually be deployed.

The exposure gap is not a buy signal. Being $800 below a $1,000 target does not mean the market is in a dip. If the market is extended and strong, preserving the remaining entry capital may be the correct action. If a genuine deviation appears later, that same capital becomes useful.

Baseline-aware tactical DCA

Ordinary DCA gives the calendar control over when capital is deployed. Baseline Farmer uses the calendar less and the market's relative position more.

The position can continue making forward progress toward its target while tactical capital remains available. That reserve exists so meaningful weakness can receive additional allocation instead of having all available capital committed before the market offers a better opportunity.

This is not an attempt to buy every small decline. A useful deviation has to make sense in the context of the market's recent behavior and structure.

The goal is not the perfect entry.

The goal is to maximize the durable value that can be locked into the baseline while it is being established.

Market structure exists at several scales

The market does not have one structural baseline. Different timeframes describe different pieces of the same market, and those references can have different shapes and different verdicts.

1HImmediate price action and short-term behavior.
4HShort-cycle direction, persistence and local structure.
7DRecent weekly movement and position.
30DIntermediate structure and swing context.
90DBroader recent trend and market regime.

These references are not supposed to be collapsed into one magical number. A short-term retreat can occur inside a larger advance. Several timeframes can disagree while a larger structure remains intact. Conversely, synchronized deterioration across several scales can mean something quite different.

Disagreement is information. The point is to see the different structures, not hide them by forcing every timeframe to produce the same answer.

The virtual baseline

The entry process needs a way to judge whether today's market looks like a useful deviation for a position that would actually be farmed. The answer comes from looking backward through the market history we already have.

OBSERVED MARKET HISTORY
↓
PRETEND THE POSITION WAS ALREADY FUNDED
↓
REPLAY THE FARMING MODEL THROUGH THAT HISTORY
↓
FORM THE VIRTUAL BASELINE
↓
COMPARE CURRENT MARKET ACTION WITH THAT REFERENCE

The virtual baseline is therefore not simply a moving average and it is not a prediction of where price should go. It is a hypothetical reference produced by asking what the position and its baseline would look like if the farming process had already been running through the observed period.

That makes it possible to ask a much more useful entry question:

If we had already been farming this market, would the movement happening now look like a useful deviation from the reference we would actually have been managing?

This is why the entry engine can recognize a market as expensive or extended even when the user's own exposure is still far below the target.

Price and volume tell the story together

Price describes what happened. Volume helps describe how much participation accompanied it. Neither one is sufficient by itself.

Market conditionWhat the framework looks for
WeaknessIs the decline meaningful relative to the applicable structural references and virtual baseline, or is it simply ordinary short-term noise?
RecoveryA strong rally after a damaged market can be evidence of a bottoming or recovery process. Strength by itself does not make the move FOMO.
ContinuationPrice, structure and participation can reinforce an existing advance without implying that the move is already late.
ExtensionIncreasing distance from structural references, persistence, acceleration and position within the larger cycle can change the character of an otherwise bullish move.
Possible FOMONear major highs, strong extension and increasing participation can combine into a late-stage buying environment. Volume can support or challenge that interpretation.
Strong rally ≠ FOMO. A strong move out of a deep decline may be exactly the recovery we want to recognize. The same strength near an established ATH, with an increasingly extended structure and growing participation, can mean something very different.

ATH and progressive harvesting

The strategy does not require identifying the exact all-time high.

As the market approaches a major high and the evidence increasingly resembles a late, extended or FOMO phase, the position can progressively harvest excess value rather than waiting for one perfect exit point.

Those harvests act as parachutes. If the market keeps running, some exposure remains. If it falls sharply, value has already been locked in.

The objective is not “sell the top.”

It is to avoid giving back everything the position has already earned simply because the exact top was unknowable.

Recycling profit

Harvested value does not necessarily have to leave the strategy permanently.

When realized profit is recycled, it can increase the amount of exposure the system is able to maintain as its new baseline.

EXTERNAL CAPITAL → BASELINE
BASELINE → HARVEST
HARVEST → REALIZED PROFIT
REALIZED PROFIT → LARGER BASELINE

This is why the capital ledger matters. A growing baseline does not have to mean that more outside money was supplied.

What the tool actually does

The strategy already exists in the user's judgment. The tool makes that judgment easier to apply consistently.

Markets are difficult to manage partly because all of the relevant numbers keep moving. Target exposure, actual exposure, available capital, previous harvests, recycled profit and market references all have to be remembered at once.

Baseline Farmer externalizes that state. It keeps the capital plan and the market context together so a decision can be understood relative to what has already happened rather than reconstructed from memory.

The machine can calculate the measurements, maintain the history and display the relationships. It can help distinguish market state from capital state and show where different market references agree or disagree.

It does not need to replace human judgment to be useful. The purpose is to give that judgment a persistent baseline, a capital plan and a memory.

Why the buttons are there

The dashboard shows what the market looks like. The buttons record what you actually did about it. That distinction matters because the strategy is not just a collection of market readings; it is a capital-management process that unfolds over time.

HeldPress this when you looked at the setup and kept your existing position unchanged. The system records the decision and the market state around it.
Bought · CorePress this when you made the normal planned addition toward your baseline. The dashboard can update the simulated exposure and remaining entry capital.
Bought · TacticalPress this when you used reserved capital because the market presented a meaningful deviation worth exploiting.
HarvestedPress this when you actually realized excess value. The decision log remembers that action; the capital ledger is where the resulting dollars and any recycled profit are accounted for.
The buttons are a memory system, not a trading interface. They let the future version of the dashboard answer: what did I see, what did I do, how much exposure did I have, and what happened afterward?

The important terms

BaselineThe target amount of crypto exposure you want to maintain under normal conditions. It is a centerline, not a price floor.
ExposureThe current dollar value of the crypto position being managed.
Capital stateWhere the position stands relative to its target: current exposure, remaining gap, available capital and realized results.
Market stateWhat the market itself is doing, based on price, structure, volume, volatility and its position within the larger cycle.
Structural baselineA market reference built from observed behavior at a particular timeframe. Different timeframes can have different shapes and different readings.
Virtual baselineA hypothetical reference created by replaying the farming approach through historical data as though the position had already been established.
DeviationHow far something has moved from its relevant reference. Exposure deviation and market deviation are separate measurements.
Dip / rebuildA meaningful market deviation that may provide an opportunity to add exposure. Being below the user's target alone does not make the market a dip.
HarvestRealizing excess value above the desired baseline without requiring an exact top call.
Tactical reserveCapital deliberately kept available so a meaningful market deviation can receive additional allocation.
VolumeEvidence of participation behind a price move. It can support or challenge an interpretation rather than automatically making the move bullish or bearish.
FOMOA late or increasingly crowded buying environment. A strong rally is not automatically FOMO; context determines the difference.
RecycleReturning realized internal profit to the system so it can support a larger baseline.
TrajectoryA description of recent direction and market position. It describes what has been happening rather than promising what happens next.
IN ONE VIEW

What Baseline Farmer is trying to accomplish

Maintain a target amount of crypto exposure. Build it without committing every dollar at once. Read the market relative to its own changing structure rather than confusing an exposure gap with a dip. Use meaningful weakness to improve the position, harvest excess when the market becomes sufficiently extended, and recycle realized profit when it makes sense to grow the baseline.

ENTRY IS THE FIRST FARMING CYCLE.THE BASELINE IS THE CENTERLINE.THE MARKET PROVIDES THE DEVIATIONS.
This is a strategy ideation tool, not financial advice.

Baseline Farmer does not promise returns, predict markets, recommend that anyone buy or sell anything, or claim that this philosophy will outperform another approach. Market measurements describe observed conditions and relationships; they do not remove uncertainty.

A dip can continue falling. A rally can continue running. A recovery can become a larger advance, and an apparently strong market can eventually reverse. The framework provides context and preserves the capital plan; it does not eliminate uncertainty or replace judgment.

Prototype principle: externalize the strategy, market context and capital bookkeeping without turning intuition into arbitrary indicator thresholds merely because software makes them possible.
