Market Strategy

How Baseline Farmer reads the market and manages exposure.
MODEL REFERENCE

The strategy is built around a simple idea: keep a target amount of crypto exposure, then use meaningful market movement to improve that position over time. The long cycle provides the backdrop; the farming process works the path between major moves.

BUILD
Move toward the baseline
Favor useful weakness rather than buying simply because exposure is below target.
FARM
Work meaningful swings
Use deviations, structure, and participation to manage tactical movement around the baseline.
HARVEST
Lock in excess
When exposure moves above the target, excess can be harvested without abandoning the baseline.

Two things are kept separate

Capital state

How much exposure you have, how much remains to reach your target, and how much capital is available.

This determines your capacity to act. It does not tell us whether the market is cheap.

Market state

Where price sits relative to its structural references, virtual farming reference, recent movement, volume, and the ATH/cycle context.

This determines whether a move looks useful, ordinary, extended, or worth waiting on.

Market structure

The market is viewed at several scales rather than reduced to one indicator.

1H / 4H

Shorter-term direction, acceleration, pullbacks, and changes in local behavior.

7D / 30D

The swing environment: whether short-term movement is occurring inside strength, weakness, recovery, or extension.

90D

The larger structural backdrop. It can disagree with shorter timeframes without either being wrong.

Volume

Participation helps support or weaken an interpretation. Rising volume is evidence, not a direction by itself.

Virtual baseline

For a new position, the system can look backward through the market history already being tracked and ask a counterfactual question: what would the farming reference have looked like if the position had already been funded before this stretch?

The resulting virtual reference gives the entry process the same kind of market-relative context the funded strategy will use later. A price below the user's target exposure is therefore not automatically an entry dip.

Reading strong moves

Recovery

A strong rally after damaged or deeply depressed structure can be evidence of a bottom reversal. Strength alone does not make it FOMO.

Continuation

A healthy advance can remain constructive when the larger structure supports it and price is not simply becoming more extended.

Pullback

Short-term weakness can be useful without implying that the larger trend has failed.

Late / FOMO

Near an ATH or major extension, accelerating price plus supporting participation can indicate a crowded late-stage move. That is a reason to avoid chasing, not proof of an exact top.

The operating loop

Wait → identify a meaningful deviation → act selectively → harvest excess when appropriate → establish the new baseline → repeat.

The goal is not to predict every turn. It is to improve the amount of durable exposure created from the capital supplied while retaining a baseline through the larger cycle.

Terms

TermMeaning
BaselineYour target amount of crypto exposure under normal conditions. Not a price floor.
Structural baselineA market reference for a particular timeframe. Each timeframe has its own shape and verdict.
Virtual baselineA counterfactual reference created by replaying the farming approach through historical data from a hypothetical clean entry.
DeviationDistance from the relevant market or capital reference.
Tactical reserveCapital intentionally kept available for a meaningful opportunity.
FOMOA late or extended buying environment; not simply a strong rally.
